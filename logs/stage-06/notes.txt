STAGE 6 - CONTENT EXTRACTION AND DUPLICATE DETECTION
tag: stage-06
===========================================================================

1. GOAL
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Decide when two responses are the same document, and act on the answer.
Three separate questions: identical, nearly identical, declared identical.

Secondary goal, and the real test of the idea: kill the generator trap on
CONTENT rather than on URL shape. Stage 5 bounded it with a budget. A budget
is a guess. Recognising that every /gen page says the same thing is not.

3. CONCEPTS APPLIED
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
BOILERPLATE REMOVAL
  Navigation, headers and footers are identical across a site. Left in the
  text, they make every page look similar to every other - which is exactly
  the signal duplicate detection is trying to read. This is not a tidiness
  feature; without it, near-duplicate detection measures the template.

  Ordering constraint: main_text() mutates the parse tree, so it must run
  AFTER link extraction. The links live in the <nav> it deletes.

THREE QUESTIONS, THREE TOOLS
  identical            -> cryptographic hash (SHA-256 of normalised text)
  nearly identical     -> similarity hash (simhash) + Hamming distance
  declared identical   -> rel=canonical, believed because the site said it

  The middle one is the only hard one. Equality-based structures cannot
  answer it: a hash table finds exact matches, and "differs by six words out
  of four hundred" is not a match.

SHINGLING
  Overlapping k-word windows (k=4). Word ORDER survives, which a bag of words
  destroys - "dog bites man" and "man bites dog" have identical word bags.

SIMHASH (Charikar 2002) - LOCALITY-SENSITIVE HASHING
  A hash where similar inputs produce similar outputs. This is the exact
  OPPOSITE of what a cryptographic hash promises, and it is the whole point.
    1. shingle the text
    2. hash each shingle to 64 bits
    3. each bit column votes +1 if the shingle's bit is set, -1 if clear
    4. keep the sign of each column
  Changing a few shingles moves a few votes, which flips at most a few bits.
  Similarity becomes Hamming distance: a number, comparable, thresholdable.

BANDING - AND THE CONSTRAINT THAT COMES WITH IT
  Comparing a new fingerprint against every stored one is O(n) per page, which
  is the cost simhash was supposed to avoid. Split the 64 bits into B bands,
  index each band separately, and only compare against fingerprints sharing at
  least one exact band.

  Soundness rests on the PIGEONHOLE PRINCIPLE: two fingerprints differing in
  fewer than B bits cannot differ in all B bands, so they must agree exactly
  on at least one. Therefore:

      max_distance < BANDS      <-- a correctness constraint, not a tuning knob

  Widen the threshold without widening the bands and the index does not get
  slower, it gets WRONG: it silently stops finding the pairs it exists for.
  A test asserts the inequality so nobody can break it by tuning one number.

THRESHOLDS ARE MEASURED, NOT LOOKED UP
  The folklore constant for 64-bit simhash is 3. It is calibrated for
  documents with thousands of shingles and does not transfer. The right
  threshold is a property of YOUR document population.

  The relationship is proportional: the same two-word edit is a large fraction
  of a short document and a negligible fraction of a long one. Measured here:
      6 words edited in 188  -> 11 bits apart   (unusable)
      2 words edited in 450  ->  4 bits apart   (workable)

FEATURE DIVERSITY, NOT JUST LENGTH
  A document can be enormous and still unfit for a fingerprint. If it carries
  only a handful of distinct shingles with near-equal weights, they cancel in
  every bit column and noise decides the sign. Length is not a sufficient
  test; distinct-feature count is a second, independent one.

SUPPRESSING A DUPLICATE'S LINKS
  A duplicate's links are duplicates too. Not following them is what actually
  kills a generated family - the chain dies at the first repeat rather than at
  a budget. The cost is real and accepted: a page reachable ONLY through a
  duplicate becomes unreachable.

REFETCH IS NOT DUPLICATION
  /a, /a/ and the end of the /r/1 redirect chain are one page reached three
  ways. Calling that "an exact duplicate of itself" is nonsense that would
  corrupt every count on the report. It needs its own verdict.

5. WHAT BROKE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. SIMHASH GAVE 14 BITS FOR A ONE-WORD DIFFERENCE.
   Two /gen pages differing by one word in 7,679 measured 14 bits apart.
   Cause: the filler was one phrase repeated, so the page had FOUR distinct
   shingles, the top two occurring 3,839 and 3,838 times. Near-equal weights
   cancel in every bit column, leaving the four differing shingles to decide
   the sign. The corpus was testing a degenerate case that does not occur in
   the wild - real generated pages carry real prose around a varying field.
   Fixed the filler; kept the finding as MIN_DISTINCT_SHINGLES.
   LESSON: an algorithm's failure mode is worth more than its success case.
   Do not delete the evidence, turn it into a guard.

B. /a WAS A GENUINE NEAR-DUPLICATE OF /dup/exact-1 (distance 4).
   They shared TEXT_A and differed only in the heading. No threshold should be
   asked to deny that. /a got its own text.
   LESSON: when the corpus and the algorithm disagree, check the corpus first.

C. THE NEAR PAIR WAS TOO FAR APART (11 bits, 6 words edited in 188).
   Above any threshold that would still exclude unrelated pages at 24.
   Rewritten as 2 words edited in 450 -> 4 bits.
   LESSON: a near-duplicate is a small edit to a large document. A 3% rewrite
   is not a near-duplicate, it is a different page.

D. /dup/exact-2 WAS REACHABLE ONLY THROUGH /dup/near-1.
   Since duplicates now have their links suppressed, one ordering would have
   made exact-2 permanently unreachable. Linked it from the index too.
   LESSON: link suppression creates coverage risk. Make it visible.

E. follow_duplicate_links WAS DECLARED AND NEVER READ.
   The config field existed, the crawler ignored it. Caught within a minute by
   the control test that used it.
   LESSON: a flag with no test is a comment.

F. HITTING max_pages MARKED A URL DONE WITHOUT FETCHING IT.  [stage-5 bug]
   When a worker hits the budget it returns without processing the request it
   is holding - but the `finally` block released that request as FINISHED,
   which the SQLite frontier records as done. Every later resume then skipped
   it. A page silently lost from every capped-then-resumed crawl, invisible
   unless you compare the UNION of two runs against ground truth.
   Fixed: release(url, done=False) requeues instead of completing.
   LESSON: a `finally` block that records completion will happily record work
   that never happened. Also: the resume test found this only because it
   diffed against a declared manifest rather than against itself.
